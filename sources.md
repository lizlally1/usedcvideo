# Sources

Unlike the sibling U.S. Energy brand video project (which researched company
facts independently and cites them in its own `sources.md`), every factual
claim in this film's narration — the Stockyards' rise as a livestock
trading center, the Armour Building's construction for Armour & Company,
the craftsmen-descendants renovation story, the preserved original white
structural beams, and the fitness center's shared use with the Fort Worth
Police Department — was supplied verbatim as an approved production script
in the creative brief for this project. `src/data/narration-lines.json`
matches that script's wording line-for-line (broken into shorter spoken
lines for pacing; no facts added, removed, or altered).

This project did not independently re-verify those claims against an
external source before producing the film, since they were delivered as
already-approved copy rather than as facts to be researched from scratch.
If any of them need independent verification before public release, that
should happen against U.S. Energy's own records and Historic Fort Worth
Inc., which recognized the Armour Building renovation with a Preservation
Project Award (see the sibling brand-video project's `sources.md` for that
citation, which was independently sourced there).

## Facts intentionally not embellished

- The narration does not name a specific construction year or decade beyond
  "the early twentieth century," since the brief's own script did not
  provide one and no more specific date was independently verified here.
- No specific craftsman, family name, or renovation date is claimed beyond
  what the brief's script states ("decades later," "generations ago").
