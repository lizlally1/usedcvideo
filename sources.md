# Sources

All company facts used in the voiceover script and on-screen graphics are
drawn from the official U.S. Energy Development Corporation website
(usedc.com) and its own press releases, cross-checked against independent
local business press. Note: this session's outbound network access could
not directly load usedc.com (blocked by the environment's egress policy),
so facts below were retrieved via web search snippets that quote and cite
the official pages directly, plus independent corroborating coverage. Where
a specific number appears in more than one place, the most recent dated
source was used.

## Company history & founding

- **Fact:** Founded in 1980 as a successor to Oilmark & Company (1978); founded by Joseph and Judy Jayson; originally an Appalachian-basin operator based in Buffalo, New York; later expanded into Texas, Louisiana, Kansas, and Ontario, Canada.
  **Source:** U.S. Energy Development Corporation — company history, as indexed from `usedc.com` (About Us) and `old.usedc.com/mission/`.

- **Fact:** Jordan Jayson became CEO in 2014, the company's second-generation CEO.
  **Source:** usedc.com/team/jordan-m-jayson/

- **Fact:** Arlington, Texas office opened in 2015.
  **Source:** usedc.com (company history, About Us)

## Mission

- **Fact (paraphrased in narration, not quoted verbatim):** "Over the past 45 years U.S. Energy has pioneered a unique project development approach that focuses on the development of energy projects throughout onshore North America but only invests in the projects that our collective experience tells us will have a high potential for success for our partners in the long run."
  **Source:** usedc.com/about-us/ ("Mission, Vision & Core Values")

## Core Values

- **Fact:** Investor First · Trustworthy and Sincere · Collaborative · Responsible to All Stakeholders · Innovative · Passionate and Driven.
  **Source:** usedc.com/about-us/ — independently confirmed on-camera from the company's own physical "Core Values" wall display, filmed at the Fort Worth headquarters (uploaded clip `53f8a416-IMG_5650.mov`).

## Operations scale / statistics

- **Fact:** Approximately 4,000 wells invested in, operated, and/or drilled across 13 states and Canada; more than $4 billion deployed on behalf of the company and its partners.
  **Source:** usedc.com (Operations) and usedc.com/team/jordan-m-jayson/

- **Fact:** In 2025, U.S. Energy announced plans to deploy up to $1 billion in the Permian Basin.
  **Source:** prnewswire.com/news-releases/us-energy-development-corporation-to-deploy-up-to-1-billion-in-the-permian-basin-during-2025-302366074.html (also republished at usedc.com)

- **Fact:** In 2024, U.S. Energy deployed nearly $800 million and completed 29 transactions after evaluating 220+ opportunities; in 2023, the company closed 19 transactions and deployed nearly $600 million, largely in the Permian Basin, with additional projects in the Barnett, Haynesville, and Powder River basins.
  **Source:** usedc.com/u-s-energy-development-corporation-announces-strategic-2023-acquisitions-and-2024-growth-plans/

## 2025 largest acquisition in company history

- **Fact:** In April 2025, U.S. Energy acquired roughly 20,000 acres in West Texas (Permian Basin), a $390 million asset — reported as the largest deal in the firm's history.
  **Source:** fortworthinc.com/news/fort-worth-energy-firm-makes-biggest-deal-in-its-history-wit/ ; corroborated by prnewswire.com/news-releases/us-energy-development-corporation-expands-permian-footprint-acquiring-390-million-asset-and-announces-upsized-credit-facility-302428356.html and fortworthreport.org/2025/04/15/fort-worth-based-u-s-energy-development-expands-permian-footprint-with-390m-acquisition/

## Fort Worth Stockyards headquarters / Armour Building

- **Fact:** U.S. Energy acquired the historic Armour Office Building (601 E. Exchange Ave., Fort Worth Stockyards) in August 2022 and undertook an approximately $8 million renovation, moving its headquarters there in 2025. The building is the last remaining structure of the historic Armour meatpacking plant complex, roughly 50,000–51,000 square feet, three stories.
  **Source:** fortworthinc.com/real-estate/u-s-energy-development-corp-two-steppin-into-the-stockyards/ ; oklahomaminerals.com/us-energy-company-moving-to-historic-armour-building

- **Fact:** U.S. Energy held a flag-raising ceremony for its new Fort Worth Stockyards headquarters on May 27, 2025, at 601 E. Exchange Ave., Suite 100.
  **Source:** usedc.com (Newsroom) — flag-raising ceremony announcement.

- **Fact:** In October 2025, the Armour Building was honored by Historic Fort Worth Inc. with a Preservation Project Award.
  **Source:** usedc.com/u-s-energy-development-corporations-armour-building-receives-historic-preservation-project-award/ ; prnewswire.com/news-releases/us-energy-development-corporations-armour-building-receives-historic-preservation-project-award-302590844.html

## Facts intentionally NOT used

- No specific dollar figure is claimed for the 2025 "largest acquisition" in the narration itself (only "the largest acquisition in its history") — the $390M figure appears in coverage but is not asserted as a precise, company-confirmed number inside the narrated script, to avoid any implication of an investment return promise.
- No unverified claims are made about the Armour Building's exact construction date beyond "historic" / "early-to-mid 20th century meatpacking complex," since sources gave slightly differing date ranges (early 1900s complex; the surviving office building itself built later, sometime in the late 1940s–early '50s).
- The company's own materials describe "45 years" of history (as of their most recent published materials); this video uses "forty-five years" / "45+ years" consistently with that framing rather than computing a different figure from the current calendar year.
